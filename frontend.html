// The Problem

Broadcast footage isn't a clean input.

Camera motion The camera pans and zooms

Following a diving catch breaks the static-background assumption most classical detectors rely on.

Visual clutter Animated LED ad boards

Boards flicker and shift independent of anything happening in play — pure noise to a motion detector.

Detection target Small, fast, motion-blurred

A cricket ball against grass and a crowd is a genuinely hard target for classical CV without a trained model.

Physics Impact discontinuities

Bat contact snaps the ball's velocity to a new direction instantly — one smooth trajectory model can't span it.

// The Pipeline

Five stages, in order.

01

Shot boundary detection

HSV histogram correlation splits the clip into distinct camera shots. Tracking never runs across a hard cut — essential for highlight-reel footage, which is almost never one continuous take.

02

Candidate detection

Motion differencing combined with color cues (the ball is white under floodlights) narrows each frame down to a short list of plausible ball blobs.

03

Kalman-gated tracking

A constant-velocity Kalman filter predicts where the ball should be next and accepts only candidates that fall inside a physically plausible gate — rejecting crowd and board clutter automatically.

04

Human-in-the-loop confirmation

On footage too cluttered for reliable auto-detection, the app asks for a handful of clicks on checkpoint frames instead of silently trusting a noisy detector — the same discipline production annotation tools use.

05

Segment-aware trajectory + physics

Interpolates within each physically distinct segment (e.g. before/after bat contact) and reports pixel-space speed and direction per segment — never a single global fit painted over a velocity discontinuity.

// Case Study

The Glenn Phillips catch, CPL 2026.

A diving boundary catch from a CPL "Flashback Friday" highlight clip — chosen specifically because it stresses every weak point above: three spliced camera shots, a hard-panning replay camera, and a mid-flight velocity discontinuity at bat contact. Three points were confirmed by hand; the pipeline built the rest.

Reported in pixel-space, deliberately — converting to mph or m/s would need camera calibration and the source clip's slow-motion factor, neither of which can be inferred from a single uncalibrated broadcast video. Fabricating precision here would be worse than not reporting it.
